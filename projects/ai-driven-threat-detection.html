AI-Driven Threat Detection in Cybersecurity

Investigating the Role of Machine Learning and Deep Learning in Enhancing Intrusion Detection Systems

An academic research project examining how artificial intelligence can improve intrusion detection while exploring challenges related to model reliability, adversarial attacks, and AI transparency.

Academic Research Artificial Intelligence Cybersecurity Machine Learning
Open Research Paper (PDF)

Research Overview

This research paper was completed as part of CY501: Introduction to Cybersecurity. I later revised the paper using IEEE-style formatting to present my research in a more professional academic format.

The paper examines how Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL) techniques are being used to improve intrusion detection systems in cloud and network environments.

Through a review of six research studies, I explored different AI-driven intrusion detection approaches, compared their reported results, and examined challenges related to model reliability, adversarial attacks, and interpretability.

The research also identifies limitations and opportunities for future work, particularly in developing AI systems that are more transparent, reliable, and effective in real-world cybersecurity environments.

Research Objectives

The research focused on three primary objectives.

AI-Based Threat Detection

Examine how machine learning and deep learning techniques can enhance intrusion detection and anomaly detection.

Traditional vs. AI-Driven Systems

Compare traditional signature-based intrusion detection methods with approaches that use artificial intelligence.

Adversarial Machine Learning

Investigate how adversarial attacks can manipulate AI models and reduce the reliability of intrusion detection systems.

Research Methodology

I conducted a literature review of six research studies examining machine learning and deep learning approaches to intrusion detection and related adversarial vulnerabilities.

I compared the datasets, machine learning techniques, reported results, and technical challenges discussed in the selected studies.

The findings were organized into a research comparison table to identify similarities, differences, and recurring challenges across the reviewed approaches.

Key Research Findings

Machine Learning and Deep Learning

The reviewed studies demonstrate how machine learning and deep learning techniques can help intrusion detection systems identify patterns in network data and detect suspicious activity.

The research examines hybrid architectures that combine different machine learning and deep learning methods to improve detection performance.

Adversarial Attacks and Model Reliability

AI-driven cybersecurity systems introduce additional security challenges because the models themselves can become targets of adversarial attacks.

The research explores how attackers can manipulate model inputs to produce incorrect predictions and examines the need for stronger defenses and more reliable AI systems.

AI Transparency and Explainability

One important limitation identified in the research is the difficulty of understanding how some deep learning models make decisions.

When an AI system flags suspicious activity, security analysts need enough information to understand and evaluate the alert. Explainable AI can help make these decisions more understandable and support informed human judgment.

Challenges and Future Research

The research identifies several challenges that affect the practical implementation of AI-driven intrusion detection systems.

  • Dataset limitations and class imbalance.
  • High computational requirements for complex deep learning models.
  • Vulnerability to adversarial attacks.
  • Limited interpretability and transparency of AI-generated decisions.
  • Difficulty detecting rare or previously unseen cyberattacks.

These findings highlight the importance of developing AI systems that are not only technically effective, but also reliable, transparent, and practical to implement.
