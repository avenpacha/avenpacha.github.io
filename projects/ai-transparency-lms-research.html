CS605 — Research Methods

AI Transparency by Design

A Mixed-Methods Evaluation of Trust and Adoption in AI-Powered Learning Management Systems

A research proposal exploring how AI-transparency features in learning management systems may influence user trust and willingness to adopt AI-powered educational platforms.

UX Research Artificial Intelligence Educational Technology Mixed-Methods Research

Project status: Research proposal developed for CS605. Participant testing and data collection have not been conducted as part of this project.

Open Research Proposal (PDF) Watch Research Presentation

Research Overview

Artificial intelligence is increasingly integrated into learning management systems through content generation, personalized recommendations, automated assessment, and other educational tools.

While these technologies offer new opportunities for teaching and learning, users may not always understand when AI is being used, how an output was produced, or how reliable the information is.

For this project, I developed a research proposal examining whether stronger user-visible AI-transparency features are associated with greater trust and adoption intention among university students and instructors.

The research focuses on how UX/UI design can help users better understand and evaluate AI-generated information in educational platforms.

Research Objectives

The proposed study investigates the relationship between AI transparency, user trust, and adoption intention.

AI Transparency

Evaluate how five AI-powered learning management systems communicate their use of AI through visible interface features.

User Trust and Adoption

Examine whether stronger transparency features are associated with higher trust and willingness to adopt AI-powered platforms.

User Experience

Investigate how general usability and participants' interpretations of AI transparency features may affect their responses to the platforms.

Proposed Research Methodology

I developed an explanatory sequential mixed-methods research design combining quantitative measurements with qualitative follow-up interviews.

The proposed study would compare five AI-powered learning management systems: Coursebox AI, LearnWorlds, Mindsmith, TalentLMS, and Moodle with selected AI plugins.

AI-Transparency Evaluation

Each platform would be evaluated using a standardized rubric focusing on four user-visible transparency features:

  • AI labeling
  • Explanations of AI-generated outputs
  • Source visibility
  • Communication of uncertainty and limitations

Participant Evaluation

University students and instructors would complete standardized tasks in each platform. After using each system, participants would provide ratings of trust, adoption intention, and general usability.

The System Usability Scale (SUS) would be used to measure general usability separately from AI transparency.

Qualitative Follow-Up

After analyzing the quantitative results, selected participants would complete semi-structured interviews.

These interviews would help explain why particular transparency features increased, reduced, or failed to influence participants' trust and willingness to adopt a platform.

Research Presentation

As part of this project, I prepared and recorded a formal presentation explaining my research problem, literature review, proposed methodology, and expected outcomes.

Developing the presentation challenged me to organize a detailed research proposal into a format that would be easier for an audience to follow. It also helped me improve my confidence in presenting technical information and communicating research ideas clearly.

Expected Outcomes & Limitations

The proposed study is designed to investigate whether stronger AI-transparency features are associated with higher user trust and adoption intention.

It also considers whether general usability may influence adoption even when a platform communicates its use of AI clearly.

Because this project presents a research proposal rather than a completed study, these relationships remain hypotheses. No participant results or confirmed research findings are reported.
